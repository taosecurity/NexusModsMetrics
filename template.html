Top modsHow these are counted

Top lists come from the Nexus Mods API, sorted by downloads or endorsements, refreshed daily. Adult-flagged mods and collections are left out of every list. Modlists are listed with the collections, not with the mods: a modlist (usually installed with Wabbajack) is a whole set of other mods uploaded as a single "mod", so it isn't one mod competing with others. Modlists are the mods tagged Wabbajack Mod List, plus a few untagged ones picked out by hand.

Trending, Most endorsed per download and Top authors come from a weekly count of every Starfield mod. Trending is the gain between the last two weekly counts. Most endorsed per download ranks endorsements per 1,000 downloads, for mods with at least 50,000 downloads. Authors are grouped by the Nexus account that uploaded the mods; the "Author" shown on each mod is the credit its page gives.

Rising means released in the last 90 days. Collections are Nexus collections (curated mod packs that install with one click). Concentration divides the downloads of each game's top 10 and top 100 mods (all mods, no names shown) by the game's total download count from Part 1.

Hot topicsHow these are counted

Translations are mods carrying Nexus's Translation tag. Franchise mods carry the Related to Movies/TV/Books/Other Games tag. Tags are set by mod authors, so a few mods are mis-tagged or untagged.

Star Wars mods are counted by name, because Nexus has no Star Wars tag: any Starfield mod whose name contains . This misses Star Wars mods that don't say so in their names and catches a handful of false matches (a modder named "Jedi", for example), so treat it as approximate. Star Wars translations are counted in both groups; "neither" excludes them once.

Character presets and save games are mods tagged Character Preset or Saved games: the character uploads often called "skins". AI content is any mod tagged AI-Generated Content, AI Media or AI Assisted. A mod with several of a group's tags counts once.

Downloads are added up from every Starfield mod once a week. "None of the four" means a mod is not a translation, not Star Wars by name, not a preset or save, and not tagged as AI content.

Forecast check

Every daily build records its pass-date forecasts for both downloads and mod count, so you can see whether they hold steady or drift.

Method and sources

Speed. For each game, the gain per day over the last 30, 90 and 365 days (whichever the history covers, at least 7 days). The likely forecast uses the 90-day speed (or the longest window available); the range uses the slowest and fastest of the windows. Every forecast starts from the newest real count.

Pass dates. The day the gap closes at those speeds. If the game behind isn't gaining, the table says "not on current trend". Sales, updates and DLC spikes show up as a wider range rather than being modelled.

Headlines. Rules write each Part's headlines from the day's data. A headline marked New wasn't in the previous report: a new story, or the same kind of story about a different game, mod or type. A headline that only has updated numbers isn't marked new.

Data. Totals come from the Nexus Mods public GraphQL API (one request a day). Download history before daily collection began comes from hand-recorded figures for Witcher 3, Bannerlord, Starfield, Morrowind and Fallout 3. Mod-count history comes from mod creation dates.

No adult content. This page does not feature NSFW or adult content. No adult-flagged mod or collection is named anywhere on it. The game-wide totals in Parts 1, 2, 3 and 5 come straight from Nexus Mods and include its adult-flagged mods (about 3% of Starfield's), because those totals can't be split.

Data: Nexus Mods public API, collected once a day. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Nexus Mods.
